# Visulaserz

An audio-reactive light show with a sound rack. Drop in music files, paste a YouTube link, or play the built-in demo, then bend the sound (slowed + reverb, vaporwave, nightcore, 8D, lo-fi tape…) while WebGL scenes react to it: a handful on the rack, dozens more one click away in the scene market.

## Run it

```bash
npm start
```

Then open <http://localhost:4777>. The app itself has no dependencies.

### YouTube (one-time setup)

YouTube audio is fetched by `yt-dlp` and converted to MP3 by `ffmpeg`, both running locally. Install `yt-dlp` into a project-local virtualenv with:

```bash
npm run setup
```

Requirements: Python 3 and `ffmpeg` on your PATH. If YouTube stops working (it changes often), update `yt-dlp` with the same command and restart. Downloaded MP3s are cached in `cache/` and appear under "Saved from YouTube" in the crate; the app only ever listens on `127.0.0.1`.

## What's inside

- **Sound rack** – varispeed (speed with or without pitch lock), convolution reverb with size and tone, echo, bass/treble/muffle, 8D orbit, drive, vinyl crackle, and presets. Press **Store** to keep your own knob settings as "Mine". The **Ambience** knob sets the level of scene sound, mixed under the music: by default a synthesised rain in the Rain scene, or any track you mark with its ☂ button in the crate (search "rain sounds" on YouTube, press ☂, done). Drop a photo onto the page and the Rain scene shows it through the glass.
- **Vision rack** – the scenes you have picked, on a strip that scrolls sideways, grouped as Show, Journey, Vibe, Things and Lab; nine palettes with a beat-synced "Cycle" option, one-click looks (Cinema, Club, Retro, Mirror, Zen), trails/zoom/spin feedback, bloom, chromatic fringe, grain, symmetry modes, an "Auto" scene director, and a "Reduce flashing" switch.
- **Scene market** (the dashed **Market** key on the strip, or `K`) – every scene lives here until you want it, so the rack stays short however many there are. Click a card's picture to try the scene on the stage behind the drawer; close the drawer and the show goes back to where it was. **+** adds it to the strip (and to the Auto director and the number keys), **✓** takes it off again and frees its shader. Search by name or tag ("space", "rain", "turkish"), filter by group, or sort by **Match** to see what suits the music playing now. A first visit starts with ten scenes; Nova is built in.
- **Perform rack** – the moves you make with your hands while the track runs. A **bipolar DJ filter** (turn left and a resonant lowpass closes, right and a highpass opens, centre is bypass) and a **3-band isolator** with LOW/MID/HIGH kill pads: take the bass out and the scene goes thin while the beat flashes keep firing, because the picture is analysed after the effects and the beat before them. **Dub echo**: hold Throw to push the music into the delay and let the repeats ring on after you release, or Echo out to make the track vanish and leave only the tail; Sync locks the echo time to the detected tempo (and therefore follows the Speed knob). A **trance gate** with six patterns and a depth knob, scheduled on the audio clock so it never wobbles. An **XY pad** that bends two effects at once under one finger and springs back when you let go — hold Shift as you release to leave them where they are. **Panic** (`\`) releases everything at once.
- **Stage rack** – hold-to-play pads (tape stop, stutter, strobe, blackout, freeze), a manual DROP, snapshots to PNG, an A–B loop, and live input: visualise whatever the computer is playing, or the microphone. Live input runs through the whole rack, so the filter, the kills and the pads shape the picture in mic mode too; a monitor gain keeps a microphone out of the speakers.
- The deck shows VU needles and the track's waveform under the seek bar.
- **DJ decks** (the turntables key, or `D`) – two decks and a club mixer in place of the single player. Each deck has its waveform in three colours (bass, mids, highs) scrolling past a fixed playhead with the beat grid over it, a jog wheel (push the rim to nudge, touch the platter to scratch; with **Slip** on the track runs on underneath and comes back where it would have been), a tempo fader with ±8/16/50 % ranges and **Key** lock, CUE and PLAY that behave like a CDJ's, and eight pads in five modes: hot **Cues** (kept per track), beat **Loops** of ¼ to 32 beats, **Roll** (a loop while you hold, then back on the timeline as if it never happened), beat **Jump**, and **Grid**. **SYNC** takes the other deck's tempo (at double or half time if that is nearer), lines the beats up and keeps following; **Quant** makes cues, loops and jumps land on the beat. The tempo and the beat grid are found when a track loads, to a hundredth of a BPM on steady music, but a half-time, syncopated or heavily slowed track can come out at half the tempo or on the off-beat, and a reading the analysis is unsure of shows a **?**. The Grid pads fix it: ×2 and ÷2, shift half a beat or 10 ms, or **Tap** along with four beats or more, after which the track's own onsets place the grid exactly. Fixes are remembered for the track. The mixer has trim (with auto gain on load), a three-band isolator, the bipolar filter, channel faders with meters, a crossfader with a smooth or a sharp curve, and a beat-timed echo and flanger. The mix goes through the rack into the scenes like any track; the artwork follows the deck on air, and the lyrics rest. Load a track with the **A**/**B** buttons on a crate row, a click on the row (into the free deck), or by dropping a file on a deck. Keys: `Z X S` and `N M J` are cue, play and sync, the number and letter rows play the pads, the arrows move the crossfader. There is no headphone cue: what you hear is the master.
- **Lyrics** – synced words are looked up on [LRCLIB](https://lrclib.net) when a track loads (matched by title, artist and duration), or you drop an `.lrc` file / paste LRC text in the crate. The words are drawn inside the WebGL pipeline as kinetic typography, so they get bloom and colour fringing like the scene and end up in REC videos. Fifteen styles (Rise, Karaoke, Pulse, Type, Wave, Scatter, Glitch, Zoom, Flip, Slam, Fall, Neon, Tower, Swing, Split) with enter and exit animations, or **Auto**, which changes the style every four lines so a whole clip varies by itself. Five faces (Auto, Body, Display, Poster, Serif, Mono), four sizes, three positions and an offset knob, all in the Vision rack. Word-level effects use real `<mm:ss.xx>` stamps when the LRC has them, otherwise words are spread over each line's time. Every glyph carries a soft dark halo so lines stay readable over a bright scene.
- **Magic** – two lists of recommendations in the crate. *More like this* is what goes with the track that is playing: YouTube's own radio mix for it, fetched as metadata only and tidied (duplicates, live streams and hour-long compilations dropped). A file from your disk works too — it is looked up by name once. *For you* blends the mixes of the tracks you have actually listened to lately: a listen counts after thirty seconds, recent and finished listens count more (`tasteWeight()` in `public/js/ui/history.js` is the one function that decides how), a song gains from every mix it appears in, and the list is spread across artists and across your different moods (`lib/reco.js`). Every pick says which of your tracks it comes from, nothing you have already heard is offered, and the history lives only in this browser — **Forget what I played** clears it. A pick downloads like any search result; **+** queues it instead. **Auto-DJ** queues the top pick once the last track in the queue passes its middle, so the music never stops. Needs the local server, like everything YouTube.
- **Export WAV** renders the current track through the rack faster than real time. **REC** records the show (picture and sound) to a WebM video.
- Press `?` in the app for the keyboard shortcuts.

## Scene guide

Every scene is a fragment shader that reads the same few signals: the spectrum, the waveform, bass/mid/high levels, the beat (and how long ago it was), a travel clock that runs faster when the music is louder, where the track is (seconds in, and its length), and the track's title and artwork as textures. A scene can also bring a swarm of points, each placed by its own little program, and a memory: a simulation it steps forward sixty times a second, so a picture can grow instead of being drawn afresh every frame.

**Show** – *Nova* (radial analyser and shockwaves), *Lasers* (a rig of beams, one band each), *Bars*, *Orb* (a spiked sphere), *Tunnel*, *Kaleido*, *Scope*, *XY* (vectorscope), *Title* (the track's name as the picture), *Pulsar* (stacked ridgelines of the spectrogram, after the CP 1919 plot), *Lattice* (a corridor through a crystal of cubes, each on its own band), *Fireworks* (the big shell is the beat), *Drones* (nine hundred drones over a harbour at night; every eight beats they fly into a new figure — an analyser wall, a globe, a heart that beats, a ringed planet, a loudspeaker cone — and the water doubles them), *Mirrorball* (a real mirror ball in a hazy room: every facet throws a spot that sweeps the walls, three lights for bass, mid and treble, beams in the smoke and an LED floor). Three are the Winamp visualisers of around 2000, rebuilt (search the market for "winamp"): *Milk* (MilkDrop: the waveform drawn into a picture that a per-pixel warp keeps zooming, turning and wobbling into smoke and tunnels; eight presets in that manner melt into each other every 32 beats, with borders, motion vectors, hexagons that each hold the whole last picture, video echo, and the song title melting in when a track starts), *Superscope* (AVS: Bass Spin, Oscilloscope Star, superscope curves, a moving particle and a mirrored spectrum over Movement, Blur and Fadeout, with Bump, Mosaic and Channel Shift on the way out; its six presets cut into each other with AVS wipes and block dissolves), *Indexed* (the Geiss plugin: a single 8-bit index channel pulled through effect maps, turned into colour by 256-colour palettes that crossfade: fire, ice, toxic, cycling sines).

**Journey** – *Voyage* (cockpit flight past planets), *Flyby* (between planets at arm's length), *Highway* (night drive with ray-marched traffic), *Forest* (an empty road through pines), *Outrun*, *Train* (the window seat: every beat is a lamp passing the glass), *Cloudsea* (above the clouds at sunset), *Singularity* (a black hole with really bent light: each pixel's photon is integrated along a Schwarzschild geodesic), *Galaxy* (a spiral where radius is frequency), *Jupiter* (eleven zonal jets, one per band, running opposite ways so the clouds shear into ribbons; beats fire lightning on the night side), *Pamukkale* (travertine terraces, one pool per band, a beat spills water over the top rim), *Nightflight* (the window seat of a night flight: districts, highways and a river three kilometres down, clouds lit from below, a firework every eight beats, the wing strobe on the beat).

**Vibe** – *Aurora*, *Rain* (a city through wet glass; drop a photo in to change the view), *Tide* (moonlit sea, plankton on the beat), *Ember* (a campfire whose five tongues are five bands), *Skyline* (towers as the equaliser), *Storm* (a bolt every second beat, with the rain ambience), *Sakura*, *Cabin* (snowed in; the bulbs on the eaves are the analyser), *Abyss* (jellyfish that swim on the beat), *Eclipse* (the corona is a circular analyser), *Nebula*, *Bosphorus* (the bridge's light show is the spectrum), *Cappadocia* (nine balloons, nine bands), *Murmuration* (a flock at dusk, each height slice on its own band; a beat runs a shockwave through it), *Frost* (dendrites take the window, one of the six arms per band, a new crystal seeded every beat), *Lale* (an Ottoman tulip bed, one tulip per band, beats send a gust down the row), *Synchrony* (a meadow of fireflies that fall into step as the music builds, the way real ones do, soft and out of focus near the lens), *Infinity* (a mirrored room of hanging lights after Kusama's infinity rooms, traced through a dozen reflections; every beat runs a wave out through them).

**Things** – *Vinyl* and *Cassette* (both turn at the real playback rate, so the speed knob and tape-stop pad show), *Lava* (metaball lamps), *Broadcast* (a worn VHS of the artwork, or spectrum colour bars), *Neon* (the title as a tube sign over wet pavement), *Arcade* (hash-generated invaders, one column per band), *Globe* (a holographic planet with a ring of bars), *Pool* (caustics computed from screen-space derivatives; beats drop pebbles), *Çini* (an Iznik tile panel in cobalt, turquoise and bolus red; each petal ring opens on its own band and the glaze catches the light on the beat), *Twist* (a puzzle cube that turns a face on every beat: ten moves scramble it, ten more solve it), *Nixie* (a nixie-tube counter of the beats, with bargraph tubes for the analyser, a dekatron and a magic eye), *Ferro* (black ferrofluid over a magnet that is the bass: past a threshold the pool stands up in a crown of spikes, taller on every kick), *Classic* (the Winamp 2 desktop in a skin of its own: the little LCD analyser with falling peaks, green digits showing the real elapsed time, the real title crawling along the marquee, an equaliser whose ten sliders are the ten classic bands read off the music, and a docked window cycling bars, fire bars, an oscilloscope and a spectrogram).

**Lab** – *Chladni* (sand on a vibrating plate; the mode changes every four beats), *Mosaic* (a rose window of folded Voronoi glass), *Julia* (the music steers the constant), *Harmonograph* (a pendulum pen whose ratios are musical intervals), *Helix* (DNA with base pairs as bands), *Cipher* (digital rain that spells the title), *Ebru* (paper marbling with the real drop transform; every beat is a drop), *Radar* (the sweep paints a polar spectrogram into the phosphor), *Lorenz* (the attractor integrated along the vector field; bass steers ρ, mid steers σ, and a beat throws the trajectory to the other lobe), *Truchet* (quarter-arcs joining into meandering loops, one band per column, a diagonal wave of tiles flipping on the beat), *Apollonian* (circles packed in the gaps between circles; each generation of the packing is lit by its own band), *Coral* (reaction–diffusion grown live: the music feeds the chemistry, every beat drops a seed, and the pattern is lit as a relief), *Ink* (drops of ink in water, carried by a current the bass stirs), *Chamber* (a bubble-chamber photograph: every beat is a collision, its tracks curling in the magnetic field), *Limit* (Escher's hyperbolic disc: tiles flow to the rim forever, each ring of tiles is a band, the tiling changes every sixteen beats).

## Make it yours

- `public/js/gfx/director.js` – `shouldSwitchScene()` decides when AUTO mode cuts to the next scene. It ships as a plain timer; the comment block explains the signals you can use to make it musical.
- `public/js/ui/match.js` – `matchScore()` decides what the market's **Match** sort recommends for the music that is playing. It ships as a plain energy comparison; the comment block lists the signals and the trade-offs.
- `public/scenes/` – every scene is a plugin: one module whose default export describes itself (`id`, `name`, `group`, `blurb`, `tags`, `energy`, its trail settings and the `frag` shader). Copy one, change its `main()`, then run `npm run catalog` so the market lists it and `npm run thumbs` to render its card picture.
- Two optional extras sit beside `frag`. `points: { count, vert, frag?, blend }` draws up to 20 000 points: `vert` runs once per point (`gl_VertexID` says which) and sets its position, size, colour (`vColor`) and shape (`vShape`, 0 a soft glow, 1 a disc); `blend` is `'add'` or `'alpha'` (see Drones, Synchrony). `sim: { height, steps, frag }` gives the scene a memory: a float texture `height` texels tall, which `sim.frag` steps forward `steps` times per sixtieth of a second, reading the previous state with `stateAt(ivec2)`; the picture and the points read the newest one the same way (see Coral, Ink).
- No build step at all: download the template from the bottom of the market, edit it, and drop the `.glsl` file on the page. It is compiled on the spot (errors come back with your own line numbers), joins the strip under "Custom" and is kept in this browser. Drop it again to update it.
- `public/js/gfx/palettes.js` – palettes are four numbers per channel (cosine gradients); the swatch, the UI accent colour and the shaders all read from the same entry.
- `public/js/audio/params.js` – every knob and preset is defined once here.

## Layout

```
server.js               local server: static files, Range-enabled media, yt-dlp jobs
scripts/setup.js        installs yt-dlp into ./.venv
scripts/build-catalog.js  rebuilds public/scenes/catalog.json from the scene plugins (npm run catalog)
scripts/make-thumbs.js    renders the market's card pictures in a browser tab (npm run thumbs)
scripts/build-portable.js builds dist/Visulaserz-portable.zip: Visulaserz.exe plus bundled node, yt-dlp and ffmpeg, runs on a PC with nothing installed (npm run portable)
public/scenes/          scene plugins, catalog.json (what the market reads), thumbs/, template.glsl
public/js/audio/        engine (playback + FX graph), fx-chain, analysis, demo track, WAV encoder,
                        the DJ decks and mixer (decks.js), their AudioWorklet (deck-processor.js) and the beat analysis worker (track-analysis.js)
public/js/gfx/          WebGL2 renderer, scene registry (what is installed / loaded), post-processing GLSL, palettes, director
public/js/ui/           knobs, crate (queue + YouTube client), market drawer, the DJ console (decks-ui.js, styled by css/dj.css), UI wiring
```
